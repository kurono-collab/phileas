It notices the country you are in Pro

Once you allow location Always, Phileas works out which country you are in and marks the day with it. A day spent in two countries keeps both.

At first, Phileas asks only for location while you use the app, and offers Always afterwards, for when you are ready to allow it.

Your rules, one number each Pro

Schengen’s 90 days in 180, 183 days of tax residency, or a rule of your own: in a country or away from it, one country or several, at most or at least, the number of days and the window it counts over. Every number is yours, Schengen’s included.

Each rule shows one number. It turns crimson when the rule is not kept, and its page shows the last day if you stay.

Informational only — not legal or tax advice. These rules have exceptions this app does not model. Check with a professional before acting on a number here.

When it doesn’t know, it says so

A day Phileas has no country for remains unknown, and is shown as unknown: it is never filled in silently. Phileas asks you about the days it is missing, and you answer by saying where you were.

The year on a globe

Your countries on a globe you can turn, coloured by the days you spent in each. Touch one to see its days, month by month. The map shows Home’s period, all your history or dates you choose, and counts the places you have visited and the share of the map they make up.

The map is drawn on your phone from data that comes with the app. It downloads nothing.

Every country counted

Days and share of the period for every country you have been to, ranked: this month, this year, the last 180 days, the last 12 months or dates you choose. A country’s page shows its days month by month and your trips there.

Widgets Pro

Two widgets for your Home Screen: a rule with its number, or your countries. A tap opens what the widget shows.

From your photos Pro

Pick photos from a trip, and the date and place of each one become a day. The app does not ask for access to your photo library and sees only the photos you pick. It does not copy, store or upload the photos, and it does not keep their locations.

Free and Pro

Free

By hand is free, always: you say where you were — a trip, a stay, a day — and Phileas counts the days, on the calendar, the ranking and the map. Exporting your data is free too.

Pro

Automatic is Pro: Phileas notices the country once you allow location Always, and adds rules, the widgets, photos and another app’s file. It starts with 14 days free, and nothing is charged when they end.

Questions

Does my data leave my phone?

Nothing leaves your phone unless you export it and send it somewhere yourself. There is no account, no server, no analytics and no advertising. If you buy Pro, Apple handles the purchase. Your phone’s own backup — iCloud or a computer — includes this app’s data, as it does for most apps. The app does not send it there; your phone does, if backups are on.

What happens when it doesn’t know where I am?

The day stays unknown, and Phileas shows it as unknown: it never fills it in silently. It asks you about the days it is missing, and you answer by saying where you were, for one day or for a stay of several.

Do I need internet?

Phileas counts your days on your phone. The map is drawn on your phone from data that comes with the app. It downloads nothing. Buying Pro, starting the 14-day trial or restoring a purchase goes through the App Store, which needs a connection.
